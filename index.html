NASA Jet Propulsion Laboratory  ·  Pasadena, CA WISTLS Sampling Drill — Design and Development Jan 2025 – Dec 2025  ·  Senior Project  ·  Faculty Advisor: Prof. Behnam Bahr

Designed a compact auger drill for lunar regolith sampling meeting strict NASA JPL requirements for the WISTLS instrument — working directly with JPL engineers on the 2027 lunar rover mission.

NASA JPLSolidWorksISRUStructural AnalysisMaterial Selection

Overview

  • Project scope and technical requirements provided by NASA JPL as part of the WISTLS (Water Isotope Tunable Laser Spectrometer) mission. Worked directly with JPL engineers Kalind Carpenter, Lance Christensen, and Kevin Schwarm.
  • Designed a compact auger-based drill to collect a minimum of 20 grams of lunar regolith while operating under cryogenic temperatures, minimal power, and tight mass and volume constraints for small robotic landers.
  • Final design: 30mm cutting bit, helical auger flights, 5mm Ti-6Al-4V drive shaft, and a protective sheath. Total assembly mass: 36.3 grams. Estimated sample yield: 22.7 grams, exceeding the 20g requirement by 13.5%.
  • Identified a critical structural flaw in the original aluminum shaft design (Factor of Safety of 0.40 — would yield under expected loads). Redesigned using titanium Ti-6Al-4V with an increased shaft diameter, achieving a Factor of Safety of 2.70.
  • Design is optimized for metal additive manufacturing. Currently in ongoing collaboration with JPL to explore fabricating and physically testing the drill.
